Bathroom Water Damage in Orland Park

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. Our Orland Park team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall
  • The room keeps a damp, musty smell even right after a thorough cleaning
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • Slight rocking when you sit on the toilet, or a dark ring on the floor around it
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold Behind Tile

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Rot Under the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.

The Room Below

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Open What Is Needed

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Cavities

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Slips Through

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.
