How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Orland Park?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Orland Park?

Typical Cost at a Glance

In Orland Park, the usual water damage bill lands in a range of $1,500 to $10,000 or more. A minor job involving clean water may cost less than roughly $1,500. Rebuilding after a sewage backup or a serious flood can push the cost far past $10,000.

Those numbers describe what is typical nationally; they are not a quote. Local labor rates and material prices in Orland Park, IL move the numbers up or down. The only way to get an exact figure is an on-site inspection with moisture readings.

Cost by Water Category

Water type is normally the starting point when a restoration job is priced. The industry sorts water into three categories based on how contaminated it is. As contamination rises, so does the need for cleaning, tear-out of materials and protective equipment.

Nationally, the category puts cleanup at about $3 to $7.50 for each square foot. Typical figures look like this.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1, clean waterSupply lines, faucets, water heater tanksRoughly $3 to $4
Category 2 (gray water)Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, sump pump failuresAbout $4 to $6.50
Category 3 (black water)Sewage, river flooding, storm surgeAbout $7 to $7.50

Category can change. Leave clean water standing a couple of days and bacteria grow, so it gets handled as black or gray water. That is one reason waiting costs money.

The Main Price Factors

The same kind of pipe failure may cost one homeowner much more than another. These are the items a contractor weighs in an estimate.

  • Area affected. Labor, equipment and time on site all grow with the amount of wet square footage.
  • Water category. Jobs with clean water cost the least. The highest prices come with sewage.
  • How long it sat. Water spreads into subfloors and wall cavities over time. Catching a leak within an hour is much cheaper than discovering it when you return from a weekend away.
  • Materials. Drying goes smoothly on tile and on concrete. Special drying or outright replacement is common for drywall, hardwood, insulation and carpet pad.
  • Mold. Existing growth means remediation work, including containment and filtered air, becomes part of the job.
  • Reconstruction. The drying phase has its own price. Putting back cabinets, paint, flooring and drywall is billed separately, and for a big loss that part is frequently larger.

Most estimates are built around two phases: water extraction first, then structural drying. In general the drying phase lasts 3 to 5 days.

Many companies charge for each day the air movers and dehumidifiers run. A home that dries in three days costs less than one that takes six.

Typical Costs for Common Jobs

These are typical national ranges for the jobs we see most often in Orland Park. Use the numbers to get oriented and not as a firm price.

  • Burst pipe. Cleaning up and drying out often runs $1,500 to $5,000, with repair of the burst pipe billed in addition. A pipe that ran for hours, or wet more than one level, costs more.
  • Appliance leak. Found early, a leak from a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine is often $1,000 to $4,000. Hidden damage makes a slow leak beneath a cabinet more expensive.
  • Basement flood. The usual range is $2,000 to $10,000 or more, varying with the water's source, its depth and any finished surfaces. Costs are highest for basement water removal when the space is finished.
  • Sewage backup. Commonly $3,000 to $10,000 and up. Sewage cleanup is Category 3 work, so porous materials are removed and everything is disinfected.
  • Roof leak. Without the roof repair itself, drying and fixing walls, insulation and ceilings is often $1,000 to $5,000.

Is Water Damage in Orland Park Covered by Insurance?

Two things decide it: the cause of the loss and your policy's wording. Sudden, accidental damage, like a supply line failing without warning or a burst pipe, is often covered by a standard homeowners policy.

Gradual leaks are usually not covered. If a fitting dripped for months, an insurer may call it a maintenance issue. Flood water from outside, such as a rising river or storm surge, is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood insurance. A backed-up sewer often takes an added endorsement.

Check your policy or call your agent before you assume anything. Your deductible also matters. On a small job, the repair may cost less than the deductible, and a claim may not make sense.

Keeping Your Costs Under Control

Most of the cost of water damage is decided in the first day or two. A few simple steps make a real difference.

  • Act fast. Water left in place goes on spreading. Each additional hour soaks more of the building.
  • Shut off the water. The moment you discover a leak, close the main shutoff, which means knowing in advance where to find it.
  • Document everything. Take photos and video before anything is moved or thrown out. Hold on to all receipts.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours. A job with active growth takes more days and costs more money.
  • Move what you can. Get rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors if it is safe.

Do not walk through standing water if the power is on, and stay out of anything that may contain sewage. No saving is worth a shock or an infection. When outside water is the source, flood cleanup belongs with a crew that has proper protective equipment.

Getting a Real Number

Averages from across the country are fine for planning and still cannot describe your home. Only a moisture meter inspection reveals where the water went and what drying and repairs will be needed.
