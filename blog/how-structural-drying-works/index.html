The Science of Structural Drying in Orland Park, IL

Home / Blog / The Science of Structural Drying in Orland Park, IL

Ask people around Orland Park, IL what restoration involves and drying the structure rarely comes up, although it is the core of the work. After extraction removes standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in building materials: drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, and insulation. Left in place, that moisture ends in odors, mold, and materials that break down. This article explains the science behind professional structural drying and why it produces far better results than DIY attempts.

Psychrometry: The Science Behind Drying

The field behind professional drying is called psychrometry, which examines how mixtures of gas and vapor behave physically and thermodynamically. For a drying job, that comes down to knowing how relative humidity, airflow, and temperature combine to move moisture out of a wet structure. IICRC-certified technicians use psychrometric calculations to determine the equipment setup each drying situation needs, including the type and number of dehumidifiers, the positioning of air movers, and the target temperature and humidity levels.

Drying Equipment Built for the Job

Professional drying gear is in a different class from anything sold for home use. LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifiers can remove 15 to 30 or more pints of moisture per hour from the air, compared to 2 to 3 pints per hour for a residential unit. To make wet surfaces evaporate faster, high-velocity air movers push 2,500 CFM or more of air exactly where it is aimed. For special cases such as drying in cold weather or very heavy moisture loads, crews turn to desiccant dehumidifiers. All of it costs tens of thousands, and using it well takes specific training.

The Drying Process Step by Step

A professional drying job is done in a set order. The opening step is a full assessment: thermal imaging and meters locate and map each wet area. Technicians use the map to place equipment and to record starting moisture levels as a baseline. The crew then sets air movers and dehumidifiers where the psychrometric math says they will evaporate and capture the most moisture. Temperature may be raised to increase evaporation rates. A technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment as needed, and document progress. The job is not finished until every wet material tests at an acceptable level against a dry reference standard.

How Long Does Drying Take?

Most residential structural drying projects take 3 to 5 days with proper commercial equipment. The timeline depends on several factors: the volume of moisture present, the types of materials affected (concrete dries much slower than drywall), ambient conditions, building construction, and the number and type of equipment deployed. As a rule, standard drywall needs 2 to 3 days, wood framing 3 to 4 days, and concrete 5 to 7 days or more. Your technician will provide a projected timeline based on initial moisture readings and adjust it as drying progresses.

Why Daily Readings Matter

Without a daily check, drying is only a guess. Nobody can say that materials are drying, keeping to the expected rate, or safely dry unless a calibrated instrument has measured them. A wall or floor may seem dry under your hand and still be dangerously wet deeper inside. That kind of proof comes solely from calibrated meters. The drying records show your insurer that industry protocols were followed correctly, which supports the claim and helps protect what the property is worth.
